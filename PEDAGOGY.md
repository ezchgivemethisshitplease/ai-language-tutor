# Pedagogy, evidence and implementation limits

This is a local, agent-assisted English practice system with explicit learner evidence. It is not a validated CEFR test or a complete language course. The current executable policy is in `tools/learning.py`; the curriculum is `languages/english/curriculum/catalog.json`; Russian contrastive feedback is `languages/english/l1-notes/russian.json`. The CLI in `tools/tutor.py` connects those components to persistent learner state. Research notes under `docs/research/` summarize supporting work and refer here for current policy. This document states the implementation limits.

## What the research supports

| Principle | Evidence and limits | Consequence here |
|---|---|---|
| Communicative proficiency | The [Council of Europe CEFR Companion Volume](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/cefr-companion-volume-and-its-language-versions) describes reception, production, interaction and mediation. It supplies descriptors, not this project's grammar sequence, fixed vocabulary totals, passing percentage or hours-to-fluency guarantee. | Keep skill profiles separate. Units use project-authored can-do goals; a grammar quiz cannot establish speaking, mediation or a CEFR certificate. |
| Retrieval | [Karpicke & Roediger (2008)](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2008/2008_Karpicke_Roediger_Science.pdf) experimentally compared repeated study and retrieval of foreign vocabulary. Repeated retrieval improved delayed recall after initial success. This is evidence for retrieval practice, not proof that every cloze sentence beats every bilingual word pair or that this app improves proficiency. | Require an attempt before revealing an answer; revisit successful material later. A copied correction and activity completion are not evidence of independent knowledge. |
| Spacing | [Cepeda et al. (2006)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/) synthesized distributed verbal practice experiments. [Cepeda et al. (2009)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19439395/) tested longer gaps with foreign vocabulary and other material. Appropriate gaps depend on the desired retention horizon; longer is not always better. | Use card scheduling for repeated retrieval. Unit-level seven-day checks are a transparent fallback heuristic, not a fitted forgetting curve. Returning late does not guarantee better learning. |
| Balanced practice | [Nation's teaching principles](https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/paul-nations-publications/publications/documents/my-ideal-vocabulary-teaching-course.pdf) recommend meaning-focused input/output, deliberate language work and fluency practice. Four equal strands are a curriculum design proposal, not an experimentally established optimal ratio for every learner. | Include meaningful reading, listening, writing and actual speech. The planner balances recent domain attempt counts; it does not measure or guarantee equal weekly strand minutes. |
| Accessible input | [Nation (2006)](https://openaccess.wgtn.ac.nz/articles/journal_contribution/How_large_a_vocabulary_is_needed_for_reading_and_listening_/12552221) relates vocabulary size and text coverage under particular word-family counts and material samples. Coverage is useful, but neither a universal CEFR vocabulary scale nor a guarantee of comprehension. | Treat lexical familiarity as a material-selection cue. A plain known-word file cannot measure senses, inflections, decoding or inferential comprehension. Ask a gist/detail question and simplify vocabulary, syntax or task support when needed. Do not fabricate a precise 98% score. |
| Corrective feedback | [Lyster & Saito (2010)](https://kazuyasaito.net/SSLA2010.pdf) synthesize classroom oral-feedback studies; effects depend on feedback type, task and measurement. These classroom results do not validate autonomous LLM correction or a universal maximum of three corrections. | Offer specific feedback and an opportunity to repair. Recheck the form in a different context later. An LLM review remains labeled as an LLM judgment and does not satisfy independent promotion evidence. |
| Practice schedules | [Suzuki & Sunada (2019)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/dynamic-interplay-between-practice-type-and-practice-schedule-in-a-second-language/C01D682D82EDBD00AF29965FCA57BAF3) compare blocked, interleaved and hybrid practice with different input/output formats. Task and transfer matter. | Teach a manageable contrast first, then mix familiar structures during retrieval. The planner's remediation/retrieval/new-material priorities are implemented; randomized, experimentally optimized interleaving is not. |
| L1 contrasts | [McManus & Marsden's partial replication](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/online-and-offline-effects-of-l1-practice-in-l2-grammar-learning/CA0DB509C645F4BF815CC6A69F6251D0) studied explicit information and L1 practice for English-speaking learners of French aspect. It motivates careful contrastive teaching, but does not directly validate a Russian-to-English knowledge base or immersion schedule. | Use Russian to explain a verified contrast, followed by meaningful English practice. The KB contains hypotheses to check, not diagnoses inferred from nationality. |
| Pronunciation | The [Council of Europe phonology guidance](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/phonological-competence) prioritizes intelligibility and communicative effectiveness. [Uchihara, Karas & Thomson (2025)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/high-variability-phonetic-training-hvpt-a-metaanalysis-of-l2-perceptual-training-studies/6ABB8C1F32D88D53EA8D05A4565E76F6) synthesize perceptual training with varied talkers, contexts and feedback. A single TTS voice is not a complete HVPT intervention. | Use minimal-pair perception and real production evidence, with feedback about intelligibility. Text and STT spelling cannot establish phoneme-level accuracy. A controlled multi-talker corpus and calibrated pronunciation scorer are not shipped. |

These sources support component principles. They do not establish educational efficacy of this particular system. No learning-gain study, outcome benchmark or psychometric calibration has been run for this repository.

## The learner model separates facts and estimates

`learner.json` stores exercises, responses, dates, session IDs, hint use, accepted answers or explicit rubric judgments, source labels and error tags. Derived skill, grammar and vocabulary summaries can be rebuilt from those observations. Migrating an old level or unit preserves it as legacy information; it does not invent attempts or mark its words mastered.

A deterministic check answers a narrow question: does this response match a declared accepted answer under the exercise's normalization? It does not prove the answer key is linguistically complete. If an unlisted nonblank answer might be valid, the result is `needs_review` unless the task declares an exhaustive answer space. Open responses need a rubric, criterion scores, a source and a rationale. The review vocabulary distinguishes an error from a correct alternative, unnatural wording, inappropriate style and inappropriate contextual meaning.

LLM judgments remain available as provisional feedback. Current mastery eligibility accepts unassisted deterministic or human-reviewed evidence; it excludes hinted answers and LLM-only judgments. Audio domains also require an audio-evidence reference. These checks verify evidence structure and provenance labels, not whether a person or model truthfully supplied them. Human review remains necessary for important open-ended judgments.

The summary reports observed task success, sample counts and a Wilson interval. The interval is descriptive under a simplifying independence assumption; repeated educational tasks are correlated and sampled selectively. It is not a confidence interval for latent proficiency, a probability of mastery or a CEFR placement interval.

## Current progression policy

The following defaults are **product heuristics** in `learning.POLICY`, chosen to prevent obvious premature progression. They are not research-derived cut scores:

- A skill needs at least four distinct items, three sessions, two study dates, a seven-day span and 80% success among the latest independent item responses.
- Evidence must sample at least two domains, including one successful production item. The first eligible response for an item within a session counts; repeating one easy item cannot wash out other failed items.
- An item reused under a stable ID cannot change its prompt or scoring definition. Merely renaming duplicated items is not semantically detectable; agents must use genuinely different tasks.
- Completing a session does not mark any skill mastered. Mastered material returns for a seven-day retrieval check; the schedule is not a calibrated skill-retention model.
- Internal level readiness requires the previous level's units plus recent reading, listening, writing and speaking evidence. The assessment window and per-domain evidence thresholds are explicit in the returned policy. The engine returns reasons and requests human review; it never automatically certifies or promotes a learner.

Initial diagnostics should identify a **working starting point** and missing evidence. The short diagnostic bank covers only its sampled tasks. Listening and speech that were not observed stay unassessed. Do not generate hundreds of supposedly known words from a brief placement exchange. Only observed vocabulary belongs in the evidence model; imported word lists remain historical claims until checked.

A weekly formative check should produce one actionable next objective, such as past-tense questions or understanding time expressions. Do not turn an informal impression into a calibrated percentage or level. Readiness for a real Cambridge or other examination requires appropriately administered, independently evaluated evidence beyond this local system.

## Lesson selection and constrained exercise generation

The implemented planner first allocates time for due vocabulary retrieval. It then selects prerequisite-eligible units at or below the working curriculum level, prioritizing recurring observed errors, due retrieval and new material. It chooses a neglected usable domain by recent attempt counts. If voice is unavailable, it keeps speech deferred and chooses usable practice. Time budgets, the 15-card cap and a backlog pause over 30 due cards are workload defaults, not universal memory laws.

The 60-unit catalog supplies prerequisite IDs, outcomes, grammar tags, vocabulary and exercise types. It is a selection index over the Markdown lessons, not 60 complete interactive courses. Agents still author the text, scenario, answer alternatives and rubric. Exercise validators reject malformed structures; they cannot certify a natural sentence, comprehensible passage, fair distractor, unambiguous translation or valid test difficulty.

Use a progression of recognition, retrieval with a precise context, transformation, then an independent meaningful response. Reuse the same communicative purpose across reading/listening and writing/speech where practical. A remedial task should isolate the documented contrast before returning it to communication. A translation item must allow valid alternatives or remain reviewable; exact match is appropriate only for constrained tasks with a defensible answer key.

## Russian as part of the learning model

The Russian KB contains 31 stable patterns across determiners, aspect, auxiliaries, questions, negation, word order, prepositions, phrasal verbs, countability, conditionals, modals, lexical choices and pronunciation/prosody. Every entry includes an English contrast with context, Russian explanation and feedback, linked units, candidate exercise types and remedial tags. The catalog contains reverse links. Pronunciation entries explicitly require audio evidence.

An observed mistake can be tagged with a KB ID when the intended meaning supports it. Three confirmed occurrences prioritize remediation. Two later successful sessions on the affected skill remove that pattern from the recurring-error priority. This is a coarse skill-level recovery proxy: it does not prove that every misconception associated with that skill is resolved. `feedback()` returns the relevant examples and Russian explanation. No substring classifier automatically diagnoses Russian interference.

`language_policy()` retains Russian explanations until supported unassisted task samples justify greater English use. Its evidence thresholds are heuristic; level labels alone do not remove L1 support. Explicit L1 requests override the default at every level. The learner can always ask «Объясни по-русски». Curriculum examples and assessed English production remain in English; explanations may use Russian to clarify form and meaning.

This schema is extensible to other L1 files. Only the Russian structured KB is supplied in this change; the existing Spanish Markdown notes remain available. Do not imply that every language has equivalent diagnostics, content or validation.

## Content audit and remaining educational work

All 60 A1–C1 units were inspected, along with the syllabus, methodology, research notes and 14 activity protocols. The units consistently contain a goal, grammar explanation, 12 vocabulary entries, functions, three activity suggestions and warnings. There are 720 vocabulary slots and 672 distinct spelling strings across levels, including recurring words and multiword expressions. They are author-selected examples; no licensed frequency corpus or validated lexical-level mapping is shipped.

Corrections in 32 units clarify misleading error labels and grammar explanations. Examples include simple versus continuous viewpoint, overlapping future forms, non-backshifted reported speech, contextual relative-clause punctuation and mandative alternatives. C1 no longer presents native identity as the criterion for idiom/register success. One B1 review example that introduced the later perfect-modal construction was replaced with the deduction form actually taught in that unit. These edits do not constitute a formal external curriculum validation.

The prerequisite graph is now explicit and checked, but remains authored judgment. The original course emphasizes grammar; fuller genre coverage, mediation, online interaction, varied voices, pragmatic objectives, a broader lexical syllabus and independently reviewed assessment items remain needed. Completion of the catalog is not evidence of C1 proficiency. Tests validate structure, references, feedback integration and selected regression cases; they cannot establish learning effectiveness or naturalness of all future generated lessons.

## Verification

Run `python3 -m unittest tests.test_curriculum -v` for all-unit coverage, exact metadata/source correspondence, prerequisite reachability, KB references, requested Russian topic coverage, audio requirements and an observed-error-to-Russian-feedback integration test. Run the full suite with `python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v` for engine and persistence regressions. Test results and remaining platform limitations belong in `IMPROVEMENTS.md`; they should not be described as educational outcome evidence.
