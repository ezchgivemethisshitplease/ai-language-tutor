# Activity: Pronunciation

Follow [the shared agent workflow](../docs/agent-workflow.md). **Mode:** fixed audio with chat responses for perception; real voice for production. A focused contrast can fit 5–10 minutes, adjusted to the plan. The goal is intelligibility and effective meaning, not removal of an accent.

## Choose an observed need

Start from a verified perception or production difficulty, then consult the [Russian KB](../languages/english/l1-notes/russian.md) for a candidate contrast. Possible targets include vowel quality, /θ, ð/, /w/–/v/, final contrasts, word stress and sentence focus. L1 membership is not proof that a particular target is needed. Work on a manageable contrast rather than correcting every sound.

## Perception practice

Present a few real pairs, such as *ship/sheep*, *live/leave* or *bad/bed*, and briefly explain the articulatory contrast. Use actual audio to play one item at a time with the answer hidden. Vary the order and context; reveal the answer and offer useful feedback after an attempt.

Record the actual responses, audio reference and replay support as `minimal_pairs` or `listening_comprehension` observations. A percentage describes only those trials, not a validated threshold for moving to another sound. Look for repeated independent success with new words or contexts. A single TTS voice is useful practice but is not a full high-variability phonetic-training intervention.

## Production practice

Use the saved voice route and the [portable contract](../portable/voice-tutor.md). Build a finite plan with word/pair repetitions, short phrases and a meaningful sentence. Include a brief shadowing chunk when the learner can comfortably process it. Define a good-faith attempt or skip as an attempted step, not as successful pronunciation.

For an example contrast, ask the learner to distinguish the vowels before using “Please sit in that seat.” For stress, mark the stressed syllable in a useful word, then put it into a sentence and vary the intended focus. Give concrete feedback based on audio and listener understanding.

STT can reveal a possible mismatch to investigate, but a transcription alone cannot establish which phoneme, stress or rhythm caused it. No calibrated phoneme scorer is included. Do not issue pronunciation scores from typed text or a fabricated recording reference.

## Evidence and follow-up

Validate any external report against its originating pass. Record production as a `spoken_response` in the pronunciation domain with a real audio reference, declared rubric and true judgment source. Audio-only KB diagnoses require audio evidence. Learner spelling is not enough.

Perception and production remain distinct observations. If production is unavailable, preserve any valid perception evidence and defer the planned voice step through the CLI. Complete only what actually happened; do not manually edit error queues or progress. Choose the next target from the resulting observations and the learner's communicative needs.
