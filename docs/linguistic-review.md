# Independent linguistic and assessment review

Reviewed on 2026-10-16 against the implementation based on `41554cf4cfa3b9ff72f28469c1d78b93d02bb12d`, followed by the corrections listed below. The reviewer was a separate AI agent from the item/engine author. This is an independent editorial and adversarial review within the engineering workflow. It is not a qualified human panel, a learner trial, standard setting, or CEFR validation.

## Scope and method

The review read the grammar explanations, examples, suggested activities and watch-outs in all 60 English unit files (12 each at A1, A2, B1, B2 and C1); all 31 Russian interference entries and their contextual examples; and all 100 final diagnostic items, including every answer key, option and rubric. Vocabulary definitions received selected editorial checks, not a corpus-frequency or acquisition-level validation. Catalog prerequisites and references are also covered by the executable curriculum tests.

The item/engine author supplied the original 30 tasks and 70 additions. The independent reviewer checked them, identified cross-modal answer leakage, then authored 20 replacement listening tasks. The item/engine author separately reviewed those replacements before integration. Thus the final listening content received reciprocal review, with authorship distinguished from review. The final bank reviewed here has byte-level SHA-256 `11952f4a6ed9f2f6542dc2156c933adc191542942f911eb593147f9eb1d662fe` (`data/assessments.json`). Content changes require a new review/checksum; this record does not automatically cover future generated tasks.

For each diagnostic item, the reviewer checked the requested meaning, accepted wording, plausible alternatives, distractor meaning, domain, curriculum target and modality. Independently authored responses then probed the scorer. The source guidance used for this process was the [ALTE/Council of Europe test-development manual](https://rm.coe.int/1680667a2b), especially sections 3.4, 5.1 and Appendix IV: review materials against their purpose, trial them, reconsider keys when valid alternatives appear, and separate editorial checks from empirical item analysis. The [Council of Europe assessment resources](https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/assessment) supply further work on rubric development and CEFR alignment.

|---|---|---|

The categorical questions/`any` rule conflicted with common café offers. | Added `I like to cook` and `I like to eat fish`; explained `some` in offers/requests. Both verb patterns are documented in [Cambridge Grammar](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/hate-like-love-). |
| A1-10 | A correction reversed a causal statement without checking whether `because` introduced evidence for an inference. | Named the intended reason-for-wearing context and contrasted an explicit `It must be cold because…` inference. |
| B1-01 | Quiz guidance told the generator to choose tense by a time marker, contradicting its own contextual explanation. | Required the timeline, present relevance and variety of English to determine the choice. |
| B1-09, B2-11 | Glosses implied that evidence establishes truth and that feasible tasks must be easy. | Defined evidence as information supporting judgment and feasibility in terms of resources/conditions. These distinctions agree with the [Cambridge evidence](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/evidence) and [feasible](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/feasible) entries. |
| B2-04 | Two purported reporting alternatives switched from a future prediction to a habitual present. | Both model alternatives now report the same increase in progress; guidance requires preservation of the original timeline. |
| B2-07 | `It was me` became an admission of breaking a window, adding an event absent from the quote. Another example invented the addressee's identity. | Supplied `I broke the window` and the addressee in the original reporting context. |
| B2-12 | A laptop repair became a commissioned service without evidence; a teacher's unspecified command gained `us`. The exam description incorrectly implied a separate four-part Use of English paper, and the distractor gloss emphasised misleading the learner. | Added the request and addressee to the source sentences; defined a distractor as a plausible incorrect option. | C1-01 | Hedging was taught as protection from being wrong and a way to sound authoritative. A grammar correction added `well`, increasing emphasis. | Tied certainty to evidence and context. Removed the unnecessary emphasis and blanket ban on direct assertions. |
| C1-03 | A normal subject clause appeared among cleft examples; another correction changed the reporting viewpoint. | Replaced it with a genuine pseudo-cleft and preserved the original present viewpoint. |
| C1-04 | A coordination example did not demonstrate response substitution. A noun-ellipsis example was presented as universally wrong. | Added a `Neither can I` response and contextualised the clear `one` model without an absolute error claim. |

No raw learner response, historical score or CEFR claim was changed by these editorial corrections. The catalog still indexes all lesson paths and exact can-do statements.

## Diagnostic findings and regression cases

The expanded bank contains 20 tasks per level. Its 70 additions supply four reading, four listening, three open-writing and three real-speaking tasks at each level. The initial version of the additions repeated the same B2/C1 arguments across reading and listening: for example, the consultation participation/influence distinction and the museum's transparency argument. A learner could infer the listening key from the preceding reading passage. Unique item IDs could not establish independent listening in that design. All 20 listening tasks were replaced with separate scripts, questions and distractors whose facts and requested conclusions are not supplied by the reading items. The new material samples everyday details at A1/A2, reasons and practical decisions at B1, stance and trade-offs at B2, and qualified interpretation at C1. These are editorial design judgments, not measured item difficulties.

The second pass checked all replacement keys against their scripts, including the changed pool time, scarf collection point, choir arrangement, translation judgment and apprenticeship interpretation. Each task has one supported key among three options at A1 or four options at the other levels. The learner view hides every key and listening script; the controller view retains the material needed for administration. No human recording, playback-speed norm or acoustic comprehension trial is implied by a script review.

The first draft also gave the same open-production rubric at every level. The final version has level-sensitive organisation and language-control descriptions: simple understandable messages at A1, connected familiar accounts at B1, sustained reasoning at B2, and precise qualification, cohesion and audience adaptation at C1. Interaction descriptions now match the requested asking, responding or confirming action. A1 speakers are not required to produce elaborate links; C1 speakers are not rewarded for complexity that adds no meaning. These project-authored anchors still need trained-rater trials before claims of scoring reliability.

The baseline closed keys were grammatically defensible in their stated contexts. The reported-speech item deliberately accepts both backshift and retained present; the present option expresses a current perspective. Reading keys are examples rather than exhaustive paraphrase lists. A response such as `The writer suggests seven o'clock` must stay eligible for review when the listed key is `At seven`. It must not receive an automatic error just because it supplies a full sentence.

`tests/test_linguistic_review.py` adds held-out reading paraphrases at every level, straight/curly apostrophe contractions, reported-speech alternatives, constrained article/tense contrasts, the actual meaning contrasts in three baseline choice items, and five listening key/distractor probes across the levels. A separate `I like to eat fish` repair checks that contextual AI acceptance preserves the exact response, the `llm` source and the absence of invented confirmed errors. Typed text supplied to a speech item remains unscored pending actual audio and review. Every learner-facing item is also checked for hidden keys/scripts and preservation of visible reading context. These tests protect specific failure cases; they cannot prove that every generated exercise is linguistically valid.

Two-option grammar choices have high guessing probability. The C1 hedging choice is transparent partly through general reasoning; a correct response alone establishes neither C1 grammar control nor research literacy. Short reading snippets cannot represent sustained B2/C1 comprehension. Mechanical transformations tagged `writing` are controlled written production, not a substitute for independently assessed extended writing. The final engine separates open-production profiles from provisional routing based on reading/listening, reports unconfirmed productive domains, and excludes all 100 diagnostic tasks from unit, grammar and vocabulary mastery. This also resolves the risk that a broad B2 recommendation task could establish mastery of its sampling unit's third conditional without eliciting that structure. Unavailable voice domains remain unassessed. These findings were supplied to the engine/item author during implementation.

The Russian KB frames L1 contrasts as hypotheses, includes ordinary regional alternatives such as `at/on the weekend` and American past-simple usage, and gives contextual rather than nationality-based diagnoses. Its pronunciation entries require audio and target intelligibility; typed spellings do not diagnose a phoneme error. The review found no further correction necessary in the 31 current entries. Contexts such as standard neutral negation and existential `there` are essential to those judgments and must remain attached to the examples.

## What remains an empirical question

This review checks content and scoring behavior on declared probes. No learner sample, item-facility distribution, distractor analysis, inter-rater reliability, fairness study or learning-gain experiment has been collected. CEFR target labels therefore remain authoring categories. A future empirical phase needs a defined population and purpose, independent trained raters, consented task trials, analysis of alternative answers and distractor behavior, and a justified standard-setting argument before any calibrated level claim.

The shipped application can support practice and a revisable course starting point while retaining those limits. It cannot turn editorial review or synthetic test passes into a validated examination result.
